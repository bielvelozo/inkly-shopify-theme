01 · Fashion

A fashion store ready to sell in Brazil

The problem

A Brazilian shopper checks the Pix price and the interest-free installments first, then the shipping to her postcode and whether the size fits. Most themes show none of it, so fashion stores pile up four or five paid apps and get slow.

What I built

A theme in Portuguese and reais where every price shows the Pix price and "up to 6x interest-free", shipping is quoted by postcode on the product page and in the cart, and a size finder turns bust, waist and hip into a size. Each color has its own photos, the grid has quick add, a lookbook puts a whole look in the bag, and sold-out sizes take a back-in-stock request.

What it means for your store

The shopper sees the price she'll pay, the shipping and her size before checkout, with no monthly app to pay for. The Pix discount, installments, size charts and looks are edited in the Shopify admin.

02 · Beauty

A skincare store that sells the routine, not one bottle

The problem

Skincare shoppers buy a routine, and most of them don't know which three products to pick. Stores answer with a bundle app, a quiz app and a reviews app, each with its own bill and its own look.

What I built

Any three products make a routine with 15% off, picked in a builder and applied by a real Shopify automatic discount. A five-question skin quiz recommends three products and says why. Product pages show the texture, the hero ingredient, how to use it, shades and a before-and-after slider, and the journal sells the products it mentions.

What it means for your store

Bigger orders without a bundle app. The routine discount, the quiz answers, the shades and the reviews are fields you edit in the admin, not code.

03 · Jewelry, in 3D

Fine jewelry you can turn in your hand

The problem

Jewelry sells on detail and trust. Shoppers want the stone up close, in the metal they picked, and a ring that fits, yet most stores show one photo, leave the size to a guess and ask for the gift note by email.

What I built

Every ring, earring and chain is modeled in code from real measurements, with a diamond shader that bends light inside the stone. Scrolling the home page takes the camera to the stone and through three golds; on the product page the piece changes metal and stone as the shopper picks, turns with a drag and shows the engraving inside the band. There's a ring size guide, gift wrap with a card in the cart, consultation booking and AR on iPhone.

What it means for your store

Shoppers see the exact piece they're buying, so fewer stop to ask and fewer rings come back for the wrong size. Each combination is a real variant, the engraving reaches the order, and the 3D only loads once the shopper interacts.

01 · Product page

Customers see their gift before they pay

The problem

Personalized gifts sell on trust. The customer wants to see their name or photo on the mug before paying, and the shop needs to know exactly what to print.

What I built

On the product page the customer types a line of text, picks the font, the color and where it goes, or uploads a photo. The mug, tee or tote bag changes as they type, in 2D or in a 3D view they can turn. A photo that would print blurry gets a warning before checkout.

What it means for your store

The customer buys what they saw. The order arrives with the text, the font, the photo and a preview image, so you print the right thing. The 3D view only loads once the customer starts to interact, so the page stays fast.

02 · Cart

A cart that sells a little more

The problem

A cart that reloads the page breaks the shopping flow, and the upsell and free-shipping bars most stores use come from paid apps.

What I built

A cart that slides in without leaving the page. A ribbon shows how far the order is from free shipping and ties a bow when it passes $50. Suggested add-ons go in with one click, and quantity and removal update right there.

What it means for your store

Shoppers see what's missing for free shipping and can add one more item without leaving the cart. You pick the add-ons and the free-shipping goal in the theme editor, with no extra app to pay for.

04 · App

Orders reach the ERP, tracking numbers come back

The problem

Someone copies each new order into the ERP and, once it ships, pastes the tracking number back into Shopify. It eats time every day, and mistakes slip in.

What I built

Inkly ERP Sync, a private app inside the Shopify admin. Every new order reaches the ERP in under half a second. When the ERP ships it, the tracking number comes back and the order is marked as fulfilled in Shopify.

What it means for your store

Nobody types orders by hand. If the ERP is down, the order waits and is sent again with one click; a repeated notice never creates a second order; and no customer name, email or address leaves Shopify. Here the ERP is simulated inside the app; a real one replaces a single part.
