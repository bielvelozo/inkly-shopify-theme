01 · Product page

Customers see their gift before they pay

The problem

Personalized gifts sell on trust. The customer wants to see their name or photo on the mug before paying, and the shop needs to know exactly what to print.

What I built

On the product page the customer types a line of text, picks the font, the color and where it goes, or uploads a photo. The mug, tee or tote bag changes as they type, in 2D or in a 3D view they can turn. A photo that would print blurry gets a warning before checkout.

What it means for your store

The customer buys what they saw. The order arrives with the text, the font, the photo and a preview image, so you print the right thing. The 3D view only loads once the customer starts to interact, so the page stays fast.

02 · Cart

A cart that sells a little more

The problem

A cart that reloads the page breaks the shopping flow, and the upsell and free-shipping bars most stores use come from paid apps.

What I built

A cart that slides in without leaving the page. A ribbon shows how far the order is from free shipping and ties a bow when it passes $50. Suggested add-ons go in with one click, and quantity and removal update right there.

What it means for your store

Shoppers see what's missing for free shipping and can add one more item without leaving the cart. You pick the add-ons and the free-shipping goal in the theme editor, with no extra app to pay for.

03 · Speed

From 11.9 s to 3.5 s on a phone

The problem

Many stores come to a developer already slow: heavy fonts, slider plugins and a banner image almost 4,000 pixels wide on every screen. On a phone, shoppers wait and stare at a blank page.

What I built

I built Inkly's home page the way those stores look, measured it, then fixed it: lighter fonts, no slider plugins, images sized for each screen, and the cart's code loaded only when it's needed.

04 · App

Orders reach the ERP, tracking numbers come back

The problem

Someone copies each new order into the ERP and, once it ships, pastes the tracking number back into Shopify. It eats time every day, and mistakes slip in.

What I built

Inkly ERP Sync, a private app inside the Shopify admin. Every new order reaches the ERP in under half a second. When the ERP ships it, the tracking number comes back and the order is marked as fulfilled in Shopify.

What it means for your store

Nobody types orders by hand. If the ERP is down, the order waits and is sent again with one click; a repeated notice never creates a second order; and no customer name, email or address leaves Shopify. Here the ERP is simulated inside the app; a real one replaces a single part.
